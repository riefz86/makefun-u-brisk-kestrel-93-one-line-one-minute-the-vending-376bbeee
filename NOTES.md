# Drafting notes — The Vending Machine Pickup Line

These are the working notes behind `main.py`: which lines were tried,
why they were cut, and why the final line survived. The final line is
the only text the program writes to `/output/entry.txt`.

## What the judge rewards (from the kit's brief, in order)

1. The line must be one a vending machine itself would say — a line any
   stranger in a bar could say loses to one only a machine could say.
2. It must use the machine's own world: slot, buttons, coils, snacks,
   hum, exact change.
3. The laugh must land in the last few words, not need a setup explained
   afterwards.
4. Short and speakable, one breath, nothing tacked on.
5. Charming, not creepy — a passing human smiles instead of walking faster.

## Lines tried and cut

- "Are you exact change? Because you fit perfectly and I never give
  change back." — Cut: "I never give you back" reads possessive, which
  tips from cheeky into creepy (fails rule 5).
- "I would vend you my last chocolate bar — and I have never once given
  up my last chocolate bar." — Cut: the punch is just repetition of the
  setup; the turn arrives too late and says the same thing twice.
- "My coil jammed the moment you walked by — you're the first snack in
  here I can't let go of." — Cut: a jammed coil means the snack is
  stuck, so "can't let go" describes a malfunction, not flirting; the
  image fights itself.
- "Press B7 — that's the code for 'come back tomorrow, I saved you the
  last one.'" — Cut: sweet, but the joke is a wink with no real turn;
  nothing surprises the reader in the last few words.

## Why the final line survived

"You walked by and every button lit up — either you're gorgeous, or
somebody fed me a twenty."

- Machine's voice throughout: the machine is the speaker ("every button
  lit up"), and the compliment is something only a machine could pay —
  a human cannot light up their own buttons.
- Machine's world: buttons, and the twenty-dollar bill in the slot.
- The turn lands at the end: the reader expects a romantic compliment,
  and the last clause undercuts it with the machine's literal, greedy
  explanation — maybe it is not love at all, maybe it is just money.
  That misattribution is the joke, and it needs no picture or caption.
- 92 characters, one breath, nothing tacked on. The "either/or" keeps it
  charming: the machine is not sure, it is flustered, and flustered is
  warm rather than creepy.

## Revision history

- v1: "You walked past and every button lit up — either you're gorgeous,
  or someone just fed me a twenty." (98 characters)
- v2 (final): "walked past" became "walked by" and "someone just fed me"
  became "somebody fed me" — two syllables cut, the punch lands one
  beat sooner, and "somebody" scans better spoken aloud than "someone
  just". (92 characters)
